    Progress against goals

    This page answers one question: how far is Hebog from the goal its implementation plan defines for 1.0.0? It is updated whenever the plan's current state changes, and was last updated on 23 October 2026, when 0.24.0 was released, the dense cell's sweep over cores was measured and a model projected the speed gate to the cluster's nodes. The scientific and performance measurements retain their original dates. Every figure is development evidence from the maintainer's machine, dated and traceable to versioned evidence and Git history; none is scientific qualification. User-facing limits are in capability and status.

    Performance The source-finding part of Rapthor's filter_skymodel step, from the task's start to LSMTool's filter_sources call, ≤0.50 of pinned PyBDSF master's on dense sectors, where PyBDSF is slow (provisionally at least 10,000 sources in master's catalogue), and no slower than master on the rest (an upper bound of 1.05), like for like on one node and, on dense sectors, across the nodes Rapthor deploys, for sectors up to the 1.0.0 envelope; each finder's growth with the source count and cores and the complete step reported beside it Every cell of the 5,000² source-count ladder fails its limit (21 October; Hebog 0.23.0 on Rapthor's thread path, four cores, emulated x86-64): Hebog's finder part is 3.14 to 4.85 times master's on the four sparse sectors and 3.16 and 2.26 on the two dense ones (14,126 and 24,780 sources in master's catalogue; the second from the full protocol, bounds 2.24 to 2.28). Hebog keeps 1.3 of four cores busy against master's 2.1, and takes about 690 s plus 61 ms a source against master's 34 s plus 37 ms, so on this path the ratio tends to about 1.7 however many sources a sector holds; even with all four cores busy, Hebog's CPU would leave the dense cell at about 0.75 against the 0.50 gate. Hebog's own profile of the dense cell (21 October, native) shows why the thread path holds it: four threads finish 1.18 times faster than one, as the interpreter lock serialises the tasks, while four single-threaded Dask workers finish 2.63 times faster and six 3.09. In the matched container with Hebog on single-threaded Dask workers, one a core (22 and 23 October, two measured runs a finder), the dense cell's finder part is 1.03, 1.07 and 1.00 times master's on one, four and six cores (upper bounds 1.11, 1.08 and 1.02), half its ratio on threads, with identical products at every worker count; past four cores both finders gain little (Hebog runs 2.36 and 2.55 times faster than on one core on four and six, master 2.46 and 2.47). On a 3,000² LoTSS-DR3 window with four cores (emulated x86-64), Hebog's finder part is 2.43 times master's on Rapthor's thread path (162 s against 67 s; complete step 2.03) and 1.99 on four Dask workers in one repetition (171 s against 86 s); both burn about the same CPU, so parity needs Hebog to keep as many of the four cores busy as master. On the 10,000² anchor, where master finds 9,276 sources, just under the dense threshold, with four Dask workers (16 October, one repetition) the finder part is 1.23 (1,249 s against 1,019 s; complete step 1.19), both finders keeping about three of four cores busy and Hebog using 11% more CPU; PyBDSF could not run the 15,402² mosaic in the 16 GB container VM. The dense 14,175² ELAIS-N1 sector took 8.5 h serially on 11 October, mostly in association; with the same settings it takes 2.0 h at 0.22.0, and 1.3 h under the compact default. On a local Dask cluster one sector takes 0.36 of one worker's time at four workers and 0.32 at six, with a 20% serial share; twelve threads are barely faster than one, and four Dask workers gain nothing on a 3,000² sector of four tiles. No regression is supported on the Hebog curve. A model fitted to these measurements projects 0.24.0 on single-threaded Dask workers to one cluster node, against master on all its cores: 0.42 on the dense cell and 0.53 at 14,126 sources, 0.50 to 0.78 on the sparse sectors, if master uses no more of a node than the four cores its sweep shows and emulation slows both finders alike; 0.75, 1.02 and 0.75 to 1.46 if master used every core. Dense gate and parity failing
    Scalability The 22,500² tier for 1.0.0, covering Rapthor's default sectors, with one sector scaling across 1, 2, 5 and 10 nodes with invariant products; 45,000² on the 18 GiB development machine and 90,000² on the cluster as later minor releases; planner bounds for 100,000² on 100 to 200+ nodes Public envelope 22,500², admitted on 19 October. Every stage runs tiled through the executor and products are byte-identical across tilings and executors. The traced peak is 1.8 GiB on the whole 22,500² LOFAR-HD mosaic, below the 2.1 GiB forecast, and still grows with the image; the run holds about 50 GiB of scratch, which must shrink before 45,000². Every task declares its memory, and the analysis itself holds about 42 KB a source and a few bytes a pixel, which hebog.analysis_memory_bytes states. Nothing beyond one machine has run. 22,500 of 22,500 pixels; one-sector scaling not run

    Functionality

    The 12 frozen public behaviours in config/contracts/phase-0-public-behaviours.json each name the test that holds them, and all 12 are implemented. A placeholder would be a strict xfail, so CI fails the day one starts passing.

    An empty image succeeds with zero sources and structurally valid empty products Implemented

    Raising a threshold cannot create a source Implemented
    Tile shape, worker count, completion order and batch size do not change the products Implemented
    No worker materialises a complete large plane and admitted memory bounds the peak Implemented: every task declares its memory and none holds more
    Rapthor adapter products for the true-sky and flat-noise pair Implemented
    Retry reuses valid stage products Implemented
    Worker loss is retried to the same deterministic products Implemented

    Dual-run mode keeps products separate and reports differences Implemented on Rapthor's Hebog branch; reports both filtered product sets and retains their originals
    A cancelled analysis stops its tasks and removes its staging Implemented, under the Thread and Dask executors

    hebog.adapters.find_sector_sources runs one Rapthor sector on the caller's executor and writes Rapthor's four files. Rapthor's Hebog branch adds the native task on today's worker layout; its distributed path is planned. The integration evidence records the upstream audit, real Prefect/LSMTool/subprocess tests and the frozen-field checkpoint under both profiles and background settings. At the pins of 17 October (Rapthor aff26023, LSMTool 156ae6a), the integration container with Hebog 0.22.0 from PyPI passes 39 native/CLI tests; the broader native/settings suite passes 94, with full branch-aware coverage of the new native module, and Rapthor's library, operation and image-flow suites pass 662. Its parset defaults to the compact profile with a zero background. The 18-case quick science check has no regressions and all 54 FITS products match the previous baseline byte for byte. That three-source smoke check did not decide the profile; the agreement check did, below. The adapter's eight-column codec reads the catalogue find_sources writes. A continuum source of one fitted Gaussian publishes that Gaussian, so its row passes the three cuts Rapthor applies (DC_Maj, E_RA, E_DEC), as a compact row does; a source of several components leaves them empty, which on the agreement check's cases dropped 151 to 168 sources pinned master keeps. Rapthor runs continuum, so its cuts drop those sources; under compact every source is one Gaussian.

    continuum now passes the truth-based gate under either background and is the most accurate finder measured: it loses almost none of the real components master keeps, keeps injected sources as often as master or more, covers 84 to 94% of the generated emission against master's 61%, finds a quarter of master's false islands, and its RMS reads close to the negative-pixel noise. compact still fails: its masks trim bright emission from large islands (5.3% of master's real extended components) and cut sources at the image edge, and its RMS reads more than 20% low often enough that it finds 1.03% false islands.     Open scientific defects

    • Clipped RMS reads low (kept). The clipped window RMS has no truncation correction: on noise alone it reads 1.6% low on white noise and 3.2 to 3.9% low on beam-correlated noise (7 October), and its median is 0.971 to 0.988 of the injected noise on the quick check's generated maps. Over whole maps on the agreement check, continuum reads 0.999 of the injected noise and 0.982 of the negative-pixel noise, against pinned master's 1.097 and 1.054. Correcting it brought the median within about 1.5% of the injected noise but lowered completeness on every truth measure, by 0.5 to 1.1 points at 5σ on SDC1 and from 0.322 to 0.289 of injected 5σ sources under continuum, without a measurable gain in reliability, so it stays uncorrected. The windows' scatter matters more: 10 to 14% of each generated noise map reads more than 10% below the injected noise (18 October).
    • Estimated background beside a giant source. With an estimated background, a crowded field's coarse background holds a giant source's emission and subtracts it, so the mask loses part of the source: 5 of master's 333 real components in the generated case and 4 of 286 on the SDC1 giant galaxy, which the mask holds 44% of, as in 0.22.0. The zero background Rapthor runs loses none (18 October).
    • Fixed meshes (deferred). Beams wider than 10 pixels are refused; scaling the meshes with the beam would recover sources to 22 pixels at about 2.5 times the local-noise read.

    Projection to the cluster's nodes

    A model fitted to the measurements above (hebog.validation.scaling_model; 23 October, scripts/benchmark/fit_scaling_model.py run step4, sha256 46340f7a…, from the ladder, the dense cell's sweep and native profiles of the code 0.24.0 released, c92c79b7) projects the finder part on one node of 96 or 192 cores, master on all of them as Rapthor runs it, and Hebog on one to twelve such nodes.

    • Hebog takes its driver time, with no task running, plus, for each round of tasks, the larger of its task time shared over the cores and its longest task; this reads 3 to 9% under the walls of every profiled run on two or more Dask workers. On a node it nears its floor, driver time plus longest tasks: natively 190 s on the dense cell (83 s driver, of which the catalogue FITS write takes 15 s and reconciliation 10 s; 107 s longest tasks, 84 s of it in component fits, extended groups and the publication of scale labels and support, a tile or batch a task) and 59 s on the sparse LoTSS-DR3 cell. The profile merges a round's repeated invocations, such as the two background passes, so the floor may be low by their longest tasks; more task slots split object rounds further, so it may be high. At 5,000² extra nodes add little: twelve 96-core nodes take 0.01 to 0.04 off one node's ratio, most of it on the second node, as the time nears the floor. Of six workers' 380 s on the dense cell (0.23.0), the 199 s beyond one worker's task time shared is 84 s driver, 36 s contention and 78 s round tails.
    • master takes serial and parallel seconds from each ladder run's wall and CPU time on four cores, its parallel work using at most the four cores its sweep shows: its main Gaussian fitting takes 1,368 s on one core (1,272 and 1,463 s) and 593 and 586 s on four and six (medians of two runs, within 2 s), so one bin of islands appears to bound it, though the VM also contends beyond four workers. Hebog's container time is 2.08 times its native time on one worker, and the model assumes emulation slows master alike. The profiles run Hebog's estimated background and the container Rapthor's zero background, so the projections take their level from the container and only the curve's shape and the floor's share from the profiles.

    Blockers and next steps

    The largest risks to 1.0.0 are the performance gap, on the dense cell where the gate binds about master's time on Dask workers and 2.26 times it on Rapthor's thread path, and failing parity on sparse ones; memory that grows with the image and the sources found; the development machine's memory, disk and container VM; upstream churn; and the three steps outside the agent's control: cluster time, Rapthor's worker layout and independent reviewers. The plan's decisions pending list what the maintainer has still to decide, each with a recommendation.

    The next actions, as the plan orders them on 23 October:

    1. Agent: one sector across the cluster. The source-count ladder, the dense cell's full protocol, the stage profiles and the sweep over cores are measured, apart from the sweep's two-core point, and the model projects them to the cluster's nodes with targets for Hebog's floor. Sizing tiles and task counts from the core budget, the first of four increments that each release, continues; 0.24.0 carries its first part.
    2. Human: decide the executor Rapthor gives Hebog, which the sweep has measured, and take the model's checkpoint: the dense threshold, the targets, the increments' order and whether per-source costs join; push Rapthor's Hebog branch, which testing and trials use without waiting for a merge; file the Astropy report drafted on 5 October and the PyBDSF report drafted on 20 October; take the other pending decisions.
    3. Agent: the deployment envelope, the matched benchmarks on the cells the VM holds, the store and planner work and the cluster benchmark's packaging; the gate itself is measured on the cluster. The remaining scientific measurement (the varying PSF) waits until this work is complete unless an output is confirmed incorrect.

    Keeping this page current

    Update this page in the same change as the plan's current-state table: after a release check, a tier gate, a new quick-check baseline or a repaired defect. The commands that produce the figures are just quick-science-check, just quick-benchmark and just traced-peak, described in Contribute to Hebog; the equivalence lane is just test-equivalence. Record the run label and date beside each figure and replace stale figures. Keep measurements in existing versioned evidence records or controlled storage, with exact identities linked from the commit. Git history preserves previous summaries.
