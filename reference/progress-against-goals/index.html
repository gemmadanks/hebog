    Progress against goals

    How far Hebog is from the 1.0.0 goal its implementation plan defines. Last updated on 22 November 2026, after the 0.26.0 release check. Every figure is dated development evidence from the maintainer's machine, traceable to its run; none is scientific qualification. User-facing limits are in capability and status.

    At a glance

    Goal for 1.0.0 Where it stands Status
    Telescopes: FITS continuum images from any telescope under a documented header contract, validated on LOFAR and SKA-Mid The input header contract is defined and tested. LOFAR (LoTSS-DR2 and DR3) and SKA-Mid (SDC1) images run, and MeerKAT MIGHTEE images fit the envelope. Beams wider than 10 pixels are refused; real Rapthor images have at most about 7. SKA-Low precursor data (GLEAM-X) needs the later 45,000-pixel tier. In progress
    Functionality: a feature-flagged backend for Rapthor's filter_skymodel step All 12 frozen public behaviours are implemented under the Serial, Thread and Dask executors. The Rapthor adapter writes the files Rapthor's step reads, and Rapthor's Hebog branch calls it, with a PyBDSF fallback and dual runs. On a Rapthor branch; not merged
    Science: a truth-based gate on what Rapthor keeps, and a held-out parity study against pinned PyBDSF master Rapthor's profile (continuum with a zero background) passes the truth-based gate: it loses 0.10% of the real components master keeps and finds a third as many false islands. Isolated-source position, flux and shape limits are met. Two bounded differences and one open defect remain. Development evidence only
    Performance: the finder part of Rapthor's step at most 0.50 of master's time on dense sectors (10,000 sources or more) and at most 1.05 on the rest, like for like on one node and, on dense sectors, also across the nodes Rapthor deploys Measured, it fails: against master 6ab4b6e, Hebog on four worker processes takes 0.53 of its time on the dense cell, just short of 0.50, and 2.60 times it on the sparse cell; on Rapthor's thread path, 1.59 and 5.66 times. Against c70103b these were 1.07, 1.74, 2.26 and 3.42: 6ab4b6e takes half as long again as c70103b on the dense cell and 0.6 of its time on the sparse one. A projection from 5,000² cells had 0.25.0 meeting every limit on one cluster node if master used four cores of a dense sector's node; Rapthor's cluster runs give it 15 cores on sectors of 14,500 to 20,000 pixels a side, so that projection no longer holds. Dense gate and parity failing
    Scalability: 22,500² images for 1.0.0, and one sector across 1 to 10 nodes with identical products Tested to 22,500² (19 October). Products are identical across tilings and executors. Rapthor's initial images, up to 24,130 × 30,564, are tested next, with no size limit beyond what memory and scratch admit. Nothing has run beyond one machine. Tested to 22,500²; cluster not run
    Release: PyPI, with portability, security, licensing, documentation and independent acceptance, under a 1.x compatibility policy v0.26.0 (22 November) is on PyPI with attestations; its release check found catalogue, RMS and mask unchanged from 0.25.0, with two quick-benchmark cases inconclusive. CI covers Linux, macOS and Windows on Python 3.12 to 3.14. Experimental 0.x

    Blockers and next steps

    The largest risks to 1.0.0 are:

    • the performance gap (below);
    • memory that grows with the image and the sources found;
    • the development machine's memory, disk and container VM;
    • upstream churn;
    • three steps outside the agent's control: cluster time, Rapthor's worker layout and independent reviewers.

    No maintainer decision is pending. The next actions, as the plan orders them:

    1. Agent: cut Hebog's time on both paths, timed natively on a crop of the dense cell in minutes and confirmed by matched runs; time Hebog and master on a real Rapthor sector.
    2. Human: push Rapthor's Hebog branch and approach the independent reviewers; file the Astropy report (drafted 5 October) and the PyBDSF report (drafted 20 October).
    3. Agent: tested sizes in place of the size limit, up to Rapthor's initial images; the matched benchmarks, the store and planner work, and packaging for the cluster benchmark, where the gate is measured. The varying-PSF measurement waits unless an output is confirmed incorrect.

    Functionality

    The 12 frozen public behaviours in config/contracts/phase-0-public-behaviours.json each name the test that holds them. An unimplemented one would be a strict xfail, so CI fails the day it starts passing.

    Raising a threshold cannot create a source Implemented
    Tile shape, worker count, completion order and batch size do not change the products Implemented
    No worker holds a complete large plane, and admitted memory bounds the peak Implemented: every task declares its memory and none holds more
    Rapthor adapter products for the true-sky and flat-noise pair Implemented
    Retry reuses valid stage products Implemented

    Dual-run mode keeps products separate and reports differences Implemented on Rapthor's Hebog branch
    A cancelled analysis stops its tasks and removes its staging Implemented under the Thread and Dask executors

    hebog.adapters.find_sector_sources runs one Rapthor sector on the caller's executor and writes Rapthor's four files. Rapthor's Hebog branch runs it as a native task on today's worker layout; its distributed path is planned. At the pins of 17 October (Rapthor aff26023, LSMTool 156ae6a) with Hebog 0.22.0 from PyPI, the integration container passes 39 native and CLI tests and the broader native suite 94, and Rapthor's own suites pass 662 (integration evidence).

    Rapthor's catalogue cuts (DC_Maj, E_RA, E_DEC) pass a continuum source of one Gaussian, but a source of several components leaves those columns empty, so the cuts drop it: 150 to 169 sources that master keeps on the agreement check's cases.

    Open scientific defects

    • Clipped RMS reads low (kept). On noise alone it reads 1.6 to 3.9% low. Correcting it lowered completeness on every truth measure without a measurable gain in reliability, so it stays uncorrected (18 October).
    • Estimated background beside a giant source. A crowded field's coarse background can subtract part of a giant source, so the mask loses a few of its components. The zero background Rapthor runs loses none (18 October).
    • Fixed meshes (deferred). Beams wider than 10 pixels are refused; scaling the meshes with the beam would recover them up to 22 pixels, at about 2.5 times the local-noise cost.

    Performance

    The gate compares the source-finding part of Rapthor's filter_skymodel step, from the task's start to LSMTool's filter_sources call, with pinned PyBDSF master. Matched runs use one x86-64 container under Rosetta, so only the ratios are matched. The 23 to 25 November runs pin Hebog 0.26.0 (continuum, zero background), Rapthor c91e157, LSMTool 156ae6a and PyBDSF master 6ab4b6e; the 20 to 24 October runs pin Hebog 0.23.0, Rapthor 632f6211 and master c70103b. The dense cell is a deep ELAIS-N1 5,000² window where master finds 24,751 sources (24,780 at c70103b); the sparse cell is a LoTSS-DR3 5,000² window with 2,469.

    Brackets are one-sided 95% bounds. Both finders' products repeat byte for byte, and Hebog's are identical on every worker count and on the thread path.

    Most of the change from c70103b is PyBDSF's. 6ab4b6e takes 1,458 s on the dense cell against c70103b's 963 s, most of the difference in fitting large islands of the wavelet residuals, and 148 s on the sparse cell against 245 s. Hebog's own times moved less, in opposite directions on its two paths:

    • on Dask workers 0.26.0 is a fifth faster than 0.23.0 on the dense cell (835 s against 1,037 s) and 7% faster on the sparse one;
    • on the thread path the matched cell took 14% longer: one pair of each on 25 November, on the same machine state, took 2,325 s and 2,042 s, while c70103b ran 3% faster than in October. Standalone controls favour Hebog 0.26.0: on a 2,500² crop, natively and inside each matched image, it is 12 to 14% faster than 0.23.0, and 5% faster natively on the whole cell. These controls do not yet isolate the full-cell task-level slowdown.

    The Dask cells above use the benchmark harness's local executor override, not Rapthor's native process-worker path; they are screening measurements, not gate evidence. Rapthor c91e157 uses one worker process per core for multi-core, non-daemonic Prefect execution; synchronous, single-core and daemonic execution uses threads. In the dense Dask cell master ran slower than in the thread cells (1,588 s against 1,458 s); against 1,458 s the ratio would be 0.57. The dense thread cell stopped at four of its five measured runs when the disk ran low.

    The thread path is slow because Python's interpreter lock serialises Hebog's tasks: on the dense cell four threads finish 1.18 times faster than one, while four single-threaded Dask workers finish 2.63 times faster.

    Projection to the cluster's nodes

    A model fitted to the October runs and to native profiles of 0.25.0, with master at c70103b, projects the finder part on one 96-core node, with master on all of it as Rapthor runs it (13 November, scripts/benchmark/fit_scaling_model.py run release-0.25.0).

    • Hebog takes its driver time plus, for each round of tasks, the larger of its task time shared over the cores and its longest task. On a node it nears its floor, driver time plus longest tasks: 132 s on the dense cell and 46 s on the sparse one at 0.25.0, against 190 s and 59 s at 0.24.0. The measured floors may read high, by up to about 9 s and 3 s: on four workers some tile rounds run three tiles a task, which a node's task slots would run one at a time. They may read low by the longest tasks of passes the profile merges, such as the two background passes.
    • master uses at most four cores on dense sectors, as the dense cell's sweep shows, and every core on sparse ones. Container times convert to native ones by Hebog's measured factor of 2.08, assuming emulation slows both finders alike.

    • On one node 0.25.0 meets every limit, the noise ×4 sector narrowly: its floor is 2 s above a target that is conservative by a few seconds. 0.24.0 missed parity on three of the four sparse sectors (1.06 to 1.26).
    • If master used every core of a dense sector's node, the two dense sectors would miss 0.50 (0.53 and 0.72).
    • Extra nodes add little at 5,000²: twelve nodes take 0.01 to 0.04 off one node's ratio.
    • Hebog's time on a node grows by 4.0 ms a source natively (6.2 ms at 0.24.0), within the 4.6 ms target.
    • Rapthor's cluster runs give master 15 cores, not four, on sectors of 14,500 to 20,000 pixels a side, nine to sixteen times the cells' area, so the model is fitted again at those settings. The local data cannot decide whether emulation slows both finders alike.
